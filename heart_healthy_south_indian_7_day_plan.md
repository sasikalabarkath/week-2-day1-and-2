# 7-Day Heart-Conscious South Indian Vegetarian Meal Plan

**For:** Adult aged 42  
**Food preference:** South Indian vegetarian  
**Activity:** Lightly active  
**Aim:** Support heart health and gradual weight management, if weight loss is medically appropriate  
**Portions:** Starting points for one adult; not a prescribed calorie or medical diet

> **Important medical note:** “Heart patient” can refer to different conditions, such as coronary artery disease, heart failure, a rhythm problem, or valve disease. This is a general heart-conscious food plan, not a substitute for a plan from the treating cardiologist or registered dietitian. Follow any specific instructions about salt/sodium, fluids, potassium, protein, and food–medicine interactions. If there is kidney disease, diabetes, swelling, recent hospitalisation, or a fluid restriction, the menu may need changes. Do not change or stop prescribed medicines because of this plan.

## Principles for this plan

- Prefer vegetables, fruit, pulses, beans, whole grains, nuts/seeds in modest portions, and low-fat dairy if tolerated.
- Prefer steaming, boiling, pressure-cooking, and light sautéing over deep-frying. Measure oil rather than pouring freely; use small amounts of unsaturated vegetable oil where possible.
- Keep salt low by reducing added salt and limiting pickles, papad, salted mixtures, instant soups/noodles, packaged snacks, and other high-sodium foods. **Ask the cardiology team for the right sodium/salt target for this specific condition** rather than applying a strict target to everyone.
- Limit ghee, butter, cream, vanaspati, and frequent coconut-oil-heavy or coconut-heavy dishes. Small amounts of coconut chutney can fit; avoid relying on it as the main accompaniment every day.
- Choose water as the usual drink; limit sugary drinks and keep added sugar in tea/coffee low.
- Do not use “low-sodium” salt or potassium-based salt substitutes unless the treating clinician approves them. They may be unsuitable with some heart or kidney conditions and medicines.
- Do not restrict fluids on your own. Some people with heart failure need an individual fluid plan; many do not need routine restriction.
- If taking **warfarin**, do not suddenly increase or eliminate greens such as spinach/keerai. Keep vitamin-K-rich foods reasonably consistent and ask the anticoagulation team before major dietary changes. Other blood thinners may have different considerations.

## Daily timing and hydration

Use meal times that suit your routine; these are examples, not rules.

- **On waking:** Water if allowed by the clinician’s fluid guidance.
- **Breakfast:** Around 7:00–9:00 AM.
- **Mid-morning:** Fruit if hungry.
- **Lunch:** Around 12:00–2:00 PM.
- **Evening snack:** Around 4:00–5:30 PM.
- **Dinner:** Around 7:00–8:30 PM, or at a comfortable regular time.

Drink regularly according to thirst and your clinician’s instructions. If you have heart failure or have been told to limit fluids, include all drinks and liquid foods according to the specific plan given by your care team. Do not force extra water to meet a generic target.

## 7-day menu

Portions below are approximate. A **cup** is about 240 ml. Rice, dal, and beans are measured **cooked** unless noted. Use little added salt and modest oil; cook sambar, rasam, chutney, and poriyal at home where practical.

| Day | Breakfast | Mid-morning | Lunch | Evening snack | Dinner |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Monday** | 2–3 small idlis + 1 cup sambar; small amount of chutney if desired | 1 medium guava or apple | ¾–1 cup cooked rice + ¾–1 cup sambar or dal + 1–2 cups vegetable poriyal + ½ cup low-fat curd | ½–¾ cup boiled green-gram or chickpea sundal, lightly seasoned | 2 small chapatis + ¾–1 cup dal + 1 cup cooked vegetables |
| **Tuesday** | 2 small ragi dosas + 1 cup sambar | 1 cup papaya or another seasonal fruit | ¾–1 cup cooked rice + ¾ cup chickpea/black-eyed-pea curry + 1–2 cups vegetables | 25–30 g plain, unsalted roasted chana | 2 medium adai (mixed-lentil pancakes) + tomato chutney with little salt + cooked vegetables |
| **Wednesday** | 1–1½ cups vegetable upma, prepared with measured oil + ½ cup curd | 1 medium orange or guava | ¾–1 cup cooked rice + 1 cup keerai/moong-dal kootu + 1 cup vegetable poriyal | 1 cup plain buttermilk if permitted, preferably without added salt; or a small fruit | 2 medium dosas + 1 cup sambar + 1 cup vegetables |
| **Thursday** | 2 medium pesarattu (whole green-gram dosas) + tomato chutney | 1 medium apple or other seasonal fruit | ¾–1 cup cooked rice + 1 cup vegetable sambar + 1 cup greens or other vegetables + ½ cup curd | 15–20 g unsalted nuts or peanuts | 2 small chapatis + ¾–1 cup dal + 1 cup cooked vegetables |
| **Friday** | 1 cup vegetable moong-dal pongal, with little or no ghee + 1 cup sambar | 1 serving fruit | ¾–1 cup cooked millet or rice + 1 cup vegetable kootu + 1 cup poriyal + ½ cup curd | ½–¾ cup unsalted sundal | 2 small vegetable uthappams, cooked with minimal oil + 1 cup sambar |
| **Saturday** | 2 small vegetable uthappams + sambar | 1 small banana or another fruit | ¾–1 cup cooked rice + ¾–1 cup chickpea curry + 1–2 cups vegetables | 1 cup buttermilk if permitted, or 25 g unsalted roasted chana | 2 small chapatis + about 100 g tofu or low-fat paneer with vegetables |
| **Sunday** | 2 medium adai + a little tomato chutney | 1 cup seasonal fruit | ¾–1 cup cooked rice + 1 cup sambar + 1–2 cups vegetables + ½ cup curd | 25–30 g unsalted roasted chana or 15–20 g unsalted nuts | 2 medium dosas + 1 cup mixed-dal sambar + cooked vegetables |

### Portion adjustment

- Start with the portions above, then adjust to body size, appetite, blood-sugar needs, activity, and any clinician-provided targets.
- For weight management, avoid crash dieting and do not skip meals as a weight-loss strategy. If hunger persists, add vegetables and a suitable protein source (dal, beans, chickpeas, tofu, or low-fat curd) before substantially cutting grains further.
- If underweight, losing weight unintentionally, recently hospitalised, or living with advanced heart failure, do not pursue weight loss without medical advice.
- This plan does not estimate daily calories or protein because height, weight, diagnosis, laboratory results, and medicines were not provided.

## Grocery list for one adult, approximately one week

Quantities are flexible estimates; adjust for appetite, what is already in the kitchen, and the menu portions.

### Vegetables and herbs
- About 3–5 kg mixed vegetables: beans, carrots, cabbage, gourds, brinjal, cauliflower, okra, pumpkin, and seasonal options
- 2 bunches greens/keerai (if suitable with medicines; keep intake consistent if taking warfarin)
- 500–750 g tomatoes
- 500 g onions
- Ginger, garlic, green chillies, curry leaves, coriander, lemons

### Fruits
- 7–10 individual servings of seasonal fruit, such as guava, apple, orange, papaya, or banana

### Grains and flour
- Rice or brown/red rice, about 500–750 g dry
- Whole-wheat atta, about 300–500 g
- Ragi flour or millet, about 250 g
- Rava, about 200–250 g
- Idli/dosa batter or the ingredients to prepare it

### Pulses and plant proteins
- Toor dal, about 250–300 g
- Moong dal/whole green gram, about 250–300 g
- Mixed lentils for adai, about 250 g
- Chickpeas or black-eyed peas, about 250–300 g dry
- Roasted chana, about 150–200 g
- Tofu or low-fat paneer, about 200 g
- Unsalted nuts or peanuts, about 100–150 g

### Dairy and pantry
- Low-fat curd, about 750 g–1 kg; buttermilk as desired if permitted
- A small amount of unsaturated cooking oil
- Mustard, cumin, pepper, turmeric, tamarind, and homemade/low-salt sambar spices
- Avoid buying extra pickles, papad, salted snacks, instant soup mixes, and high-sodium seasoning packets for this plan

## Simple preparation routine

1. Soak and batch-cook pulses/beans safely; refrigerate promptly and use within a safe period, or freeze portions.
2. Prepare a low-salt sambar base and use it across several meals; vary vegetables and pulses.
3. Wash and cut vegetables in advance and refrigerate safely.
4. Make adai/pesarattu batter or portion dosa batter ahead of time.
5. Measure oil with a spoon; flavour foods with lemon, herbs, ginger, garlic, pepper, and spices rather than extra salt.
6. Check packaged-food labels for sodium/salt, saturated fat, and added sugar.

## Important checks with the cardiology team

Ask the treating clinician or cardiac dietitian to confirm:

- The appropriate salt/sodium target for the diagnosis and blood pressure.
- Whether there is a fluid limit, and exactly how drinks and liquid foods should be counted.
- Whether potassium, kidney function, diabetes, or any medicine changes food choices.
- Whether this person should be trying to lose weight, and what a safe target is.
- Whether any food–medicine guidance applies, especially if taking warfarin or a cholesterol medicine that interacts with grapefruit.

**Get emergency medical help for new or severe chest pain/pressure, severe shortness of breath, fainting, or symptoms the cardiac team has identified as an emergency.** A meal plan is not treatment for an acute heart problem.
